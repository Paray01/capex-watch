Capex Cycle Watch

A handful of companies are spending enormous sums building data centres for AI. This page tracks one question: is that spending still being paid for by the money AI brings in?

As long as it is, the building continues. If it stops, the spending gets cut — and because chip factories, builders and lenders have all planned around it continuing, a cut spreads quickly. That is what people mean by the AI bubble bursting.

      The main number

      Is spending growing faster than income?

      If a company's spending grows 80% while its income grows 18%, the gap has to be covered by something: savings, borrowing, or the belief that the income will catch up later. The wider that gap gets, and the longer it stays wide, the more the whole thing depends on a forecast being right.

      How the verdict is worked out

      Four rules, so single odd readings do not set off alarms

      1. A group counts, not a single number. A group turns red when two of its four sensors are turning, or one is turning and two are on watch.
      2. The stage rises when two different groups turn red. Chip prices, factory output and delivery times all measure the same shortage. If they move together that is one piece of news, not three. Two separate groups moving is two pieces of news.
      3. A change must last about a month before it counts. Every reading is saved daily, and a sensor is scored at the milder of today's value and the value thirty days ago. One strange week changes nothing.
      4. Devices sold, not money spent. If prices rise 20% and sales fall 20%, the revenue headline looks unchanged while a fifth of the market has disappeared. Anything about consumers here is counted in devices.

      Two judgements a program cannot make, and where a person still has to look:

      • Why something was cut. Spending cut because customers stopped buying is a warning sign. Spending cut because there is not enough electricity, or the building permit was refused, or the chips are sold out, means the opposite: demand is larger than what can be built.
      • When to relax again. One good quarter does not undo a warning. The same patience applies in both directions, otherwise the page ends up permanently alarmed, which is no better than never being alarmed.

      Common false alarms

      Things that look bad in headlines but are not on this page

      • Nvidia's share price falls 30%. That can happen simply because growth came in at 40% instead of the 50% investors hoped for. Nothing about the actual building of data centres has changed. No share price appears anywhere on this page, on purpose.
      • One company pauses a data-centre lease. They rearrange plans constantly. That is why the stage needs two separate groups to turn, not one company's decision.
      • Last year's AI chips get much cheaper. Renting an H100 fell by roughly two thirds from its peak, then rose about 40% again during 2026. Both moves were about which chip people wanted, not whether they wanted chips. Only the current generation is tracked here.
      • Memory prices fall. If new factories have just opened, cheaper memory is the shortage being solved, not demand disappearing. That is why factory output is shown alongside prices.
      • A company's profit drops because it is spending. Money spent on buildings shows up as lower profit — that is simply what investing looks like. Amazon's profits looked like this for years while it built its cloud business.

      The other possibility

      What would show the worry is unfounded

      A page that only collects bad news will always find bad news. These are the specific things that would mean there is nothing to watch for:

      • AI gets cheaper to run faster than people use more of it. Then the shortage resolves itself, and prices for ordinary computers go back to normal without anything breaking. Watch what the AI companies charge per million words processed, and how much work each new chip does per unit of electricity.
      • Income catches up with spending. Two quarters in a row, at two or more of the big buyers, where income grows faster than spending. That is the building paying for itself, and it ends the question.
      • New chip factories open. Extra capacity arriving through 2027 that brings memory prices back down while AI demand stays strong would mean the shortage was a timing problem, not a permanent shift of hardware away from ordinary buyers.
      • People keep buying at the higher prices. If PC, phone and console sales hold up, the step where expensive hardware reduces demand simply does not happen.

      Words used here

      Everything you need to read the page

      TermWhat it means
      CapexShort for capital expenditure: money spent on buildings and equipment rather than day-to-day running costs. Here it is almost entirely data centres and AI chips.
      HyperscalerThe handful of companies that own most of the world's large data centres: Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta and Oracle.
      Operating cash flowThe cash a business generates from normal trading, before it spends anything on buildings. If capex is larger than this, the difference has to be borrowed or taken from reserves.
      The gapHow many percentage points faster spending is growing than income. Positive means spending is running ahead.
      Basis point (bp)One hundredth of a percent. 273bp is 2.73%. Used for what lenders charge above the safest possible loan — higher means more perceived risk.
      Spot vs contract priceSpot is what you pay today for immediate delivery. Contract is agreed months ahead. Spot moves first, so it warns earlier.
      Last four quartersThe most recent twelve months added together, rather than a single quarter. Seasonal ups and downs cancel out.
      Year on yearCompared with the same period twelve months earlier, not with last month.
