Capex Cycle Watch

Sixteen sensors for one question: is the AI build-out still being paid for by the money it earns? Eight read themselves from public filings and live markets. Eight need a human, and say so.

Not one price on this board is a stock price. Equities move on expectations; the physical and financial plumbing moves on commitments, and commitments turn first.

If you read one number a month

The coverage gap

Every other sensor describes a symptom. This one describes the disease: whether the marginal euro of infrastructure brings back more than a euro of revenue.

coverage gap = capex growth (% YoY) − revenue growth (% YoY)

A negative gap means monetisation is outrunning the build and the cycle can run for years. A large gap, sustained across three consecutive quarters at more than one buyer, is the crash thesis becoming arithmetic. Live from the cash-flow statements in each company's latest SEC filing:

The board

Four clusters, sixteen sensors

Sensors are grouped by where in the chain they sit, and that grouping is the point. Four red lights inside one cluster are one signal wearing four hats — memory, GPUs, fabs and lead times all measure the same tightness. Two red lights in two different clusters are genuinely two signals. The verdict counts clusters, never sensors.

Reading rules

Six rules that stop the board crying wolf

An indicator list without rules of interpretation fires every quarter, and after the third false alarm you stop looking at it. These rules exist to make the board boring most of the time. The first four are enforced in code, not left to willpower.

RULE 01 · IN CODE

Two readings, or it did not happen

A sensor's move counts only once it has held for about a month. The fetcher snapshots every state daily, and the verdict scores each sensor at the milder of today's reading and the reading thirty days ago.

RULE 02 · IN CODE

Two clusters, or it is not a cycle

Stages escalate on the number of clusters that have turned, never on the number of sensors. One cluster turning red is a sector problem — memory gluts and data-centre delays happen inside healthy cycles all the time.

RULE 03 · IN CODE

Units, never revenue

Price rises hide volume collapse. 100m units at €1,000 and 80m at €1,200 are the same headline and completely different economies. Consumer sensors are counted in units, and the price index used is one that normally falls.

RULE 04 · IN CODE

Current generation only

A rental price carries information only for the generation people are actually buying. The GPU feed tracks H200 and B200; last year's silicon getting cheap is the value cascade working, not the market failing.

RULE 05 · HUMAN

Ask why, not just whether

Capex cut because demand fell is the signal. Capex cut because the grid, the permits or the chips are not there is the opposite — it means demand exceeds what can be built. No feed can read intent; the stated reason is half the reading.

RULE 06 · HUMAN

Downgrade with the same discipline

The rules apply on the way back too. One reassuring quarter does not clear a red cluster. Boards that escalate fast and de-escalate slowly become permanent alarm, which is the same as no alarm.

Deliberately not on this board

Five things that look like the crash and are not

  • Nvidia's share price falling 30%. That happens when growth comes in at 40% instead of 50%. It reprices expectations; it does not change the physical cycle. No share price appears anywhere here, and that is deliberate.
  • One hyperscaler pausing leases. Individual firms re-sequence capacity constantly. Rule 02 exists for exactly this.
  • Last-generation accelerators getting cheap. H100 rental prices fell roughly 64–75% from launch-era highs, then rose about 40% again into 2026 as capacity tightened. Both moves were about which generation people wanted, not whether they wanted compute.
  • Memory prices falling because new fabs came online. That is supply answering demand, the healthy resolution. Check wafer starts before reading a price fall as weakness — the semiconductor production series on this board is there for that.
  • A quarter of margin compression at a company spending on AI. Investment showing up in the profit and loss account is what investment does. Amazon looked like this for a decade. It matters only alongside the coverage gap.

The other side

What would show the thesis is wrong

A watch list built only from confirming evidence will always confirm. These are the readings that should make you put the board away, and they are as concrete as the crash signals.

  • Efficiency outruns demand. If cost per unit of AI output falls faster than usage grows, the hardware scarcity dissolves without a bust. Watch price per million tokens at the frontier labs and performance per watt at each new accelerator generation.
  • The coverage gap goes negative. Two consecutive quarters at two or more large buyers where revenue growth exceeds capex growth. That is the build-out paying for itself, and it retires the whole thesis. Nvidia's own gap on the table above is already negative — it is the seller, not the buyer, which is the point.
  • Memory supply responds. New fab capacity landing through 2027 that re-normalises consumer DRAM and NAND while AI demand stays strong. The squeeze would then have been a timing problem, not a structural transfer of hardware from households to data centres.
  • Consumers absorb the price. If PC, phone and console units hold up at the higher prices, the demand-destruction step simply does not happen, and hardware inflation stays an inconvenience rather than a mechanism.

You do not have to remember to look. When a sensor changes state, when the stage moves, or when a manual assessment passes 45 days, the job raises a macOS notification and appends the change to alerts.log. On an ordinary day it says nothing at all, which is the point — the board is designed to be boring until it is not.
