    The main number

    Is spending growing faster than income?

    If a company's spending grows 80% while its income grows 18%, something has to cover the difference: savings, borrowing, or the belief that income will catch up. The wider that gap, and the longer it lasts, the more the whole thing rests on a forecast being right.

    gap = spending growth − income growth

    Method

    How the verdict is worked out

    Four rules, so that one odd reading never sets off an alarm.

    1. A group counts, not a single reading. A group turns red when two of its four readings are turning, or one is turning and two are on watch.
    2. The stage rises only when two different groups turn red. Chip prices, factory output and delivery times all measure the same shortage. Moving together, they are one piece of news, not three. Two separate groups moving is two pieces of news.
    3. A change must last about a month. Every reading is saved daily, and each one is scored at the milder of today's value and the value thirty days ago. A single strange week changes nothing.
    4. Devices sold, not money taken. If prices rise 20% while sales fall 20%, revenue looks unchanged though a fifth of the market has gone. Anything about consumers here is counted in devices.

    Two judgements no program can make:

    • Why something was cut. Spending cut because customers stopped buying is a warning. Spending cut because there is not enough electricity, the permit was refused, or the chips are sold out means the opposite — demand is larger than what can be built.
    • When to relax again. One good quarter does not undo a warning. The same patience applies in both directions; otherwise the page ends up permanently alarmed, which is no more useful than never being alarmed.

    Not on this page

    Five things that look bad but are not

    • Nvidia's share price falls 30%. That can happen because growth came in at 40% instead of the hoped-for 50%. Nothing about the actual building has changed. No share price appears anywhere here, on purpose.
    • One company pauses a data-centre lease. They rearrange plans constantly, which is why the stage needs two separate groups to turn.
    • Last year's AI chips get cheaper. Renting an H100 fell by roughly two thirds from its peak, then rose about 40% again in 2026. Both moves were about which chip people wanted, not whether they wanted chips.
    • Memory prices fall. If new factories just opened, cheaper memory is the shortage being solved. That is why factory output sits next to the price.
    • Profit drops because a company is spending. Money spent on buildings shows up as lower profit — that is what investing looks like. Amazon's profits looked like this for years.

    The other side

    What would show the worry is unfounded

    A page that only collects bad news will always find bad news. These are the specific readings that would end the question.

    • AI gets cheaper to run faster than people use more of it. Then the shortage resolves itself and ordinary computer prices return to normal without anything breaking. Watch the price per million words processed, and the work each new chip does per unit of electricity.
    • Income catches up. Two quarters running, at two or more of the big buyers, where income grows faster than spending. Nvidia's line in the table already looks like this — but it sells the equipment rather than buying it.
    • New chip factories open. Capacity arriving through 2027 that brings memory prices down while AI demand stays strong would mean the shortage was a timing problem, not a permanent shift away from ordinary buyers.
    • People keep buying at the higher prices. If PC, phone and console sales hold up, the step where expensive hardware reduces demand simply never happens.

    Glossary

    Everything you need to read this page

    Capex
    Capital expenditure: money spent on buildings and equipment rather than day-to-day running costs. Here it is almost entirely data centres and AI chips.
    Hyperscaler
    The handful of companies that own most of the world's large data centres: Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta and Oracle.
    Operating cash flow
    The cash a business makes from normal trading, before it spends anything on buildings. If capex exceeds it, the difference is borrowed or taken from reserves.
    The gap
    How many percentage points faster spending grows than income. Positive means spending is running ahead.
    Basis point
    One hundredth of a percent, so 273bp is 2.73%. Used for the extra interest lenders charge above the safest loan; higher means more perceived risk.
    Spot vs contract
    Spot is what you pay today for immediate delivery. Contract is agreed months ahead. Spot moves first, so it warns earlier.
    Last four quarters
    The most recent twelve months added together rather than a single quarter, so seasonal swings cancel out.
    Year on year
    Compared with the same period twelve months earlier, not with last month.

    Where the figures come from. Company filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, for spending, cash flow and income. The Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, for lending costs and official price statistics. The Vast.ai marketplace, for live AI chip rental prices. All public, all free.

    How it stays current. A small program runs twice a day on a Mac, takes about a minute, and republishes everything. It sends a notification if any reading changes state, so there is nothing to remember. On an ordinary day it says nothing.

    A way of reading public data, not financial advice. The automatic readings are mechanical and can be wrong in ways a person would spot. Every threshold is written out above, so you can disagree with any of them.
